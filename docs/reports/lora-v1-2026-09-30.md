### Three findings, in order of how much they matter

**1. It failed on golf, which is the content this persona exists to make.**
`gen_04` is the only prompt that put her in golf clothing on a course, and it produced a
**visibly different person** — blonde, different face, different bone structure. It
scored 0.94037 and the gate rejected it. The other seven prompts are portrait-ish and
all held.

That is the same shape as the spike's finding about `veo3.1`: identity survives inside
the composition the training data covers and drifts when the prompt pulls away from it.
Her reference set is 89 of 102 portrait stills. **The LoRA learned a portrait of her, not
a person.**

**2. The scorer agreed with my eye on both images I checked.** I looked at the weakest
pass (`gen_05`, 0.96909) and the fail (`gen_04`). The pass is unmistakably the same woman
as the control portraits; the fail is unmistakably not. After Gate A §3 — *"the scorer
passes clips a human rejects"* — this is worth recording: on a **still with a face in it,
asked whether it is her**, the gate answered correctly twice. It is the third true
positive in the project's history and the first outside video.

**3. The margin is thin and the gap to the ceiling is real.** Passing scores cluster
0.969–0.975 against a 0.98431 ceiling and a 0.9619 threshold. The LoRA sits roughly
**two thirds of the way** from the threshold to the ceiling. That is a working likeness,
not a strong one, and the fail sits only 0.02 below the weakest pass.

## What this does not tell us

- **Eight images, one seed each, one scale (1.0).** No repeat takes, so the failure rate
  on golf prompts is 1 of 1 rather than a measured rate.
- **1000 steps only.** 2000 was costed at $48 and not run. Whether the gap to the ceiling
  is a step-count problem or a training-data problem is untested — and finding 1 suggests
  it is the data.
- **Nothing about video.** This is stills. The identity question that matters is whether
  the face survives animation.

## Follow-up, same day: steps 1 and 2 run

**Both cheap probes were run, at $0.70 in total, and the second one worked.**

### Step 1 — is the golf failure reliable? Yes.

Three more takes of the golf prompt at scale 1.0:

| | |
|---|---|
| Passed | **1 of 3** |
| Mean | 0.95905 — *below* the 0.9619 threshold |
| Min | 0.95151 |

With the original take that is **1 pass in 4**. Not an unlucky draw; the prompt is
reliably bad at this strength.

### Step 2 — does more LoRA weight fix it? Largely, yes.

Same prompt, same seed policy, scale **1.3**:

| Prompt set | scale 1.0 | scale 1.3 |
|---|---|---|
| Golf course, 3 takes | 1/3 pass, **0.95905** mean | **2/3 pass, 0.97344** mean |
| Portraits, 4 prompts | 0.97212 mean | **4/4 pass, 0.97948** mean |

Ceiling is 0.98431.

**It is not a trade-off.** The worry with raising LoRA weight is that it buys likeness by
destroying prompt control, so the portraits were re-run as the check — and they got
*better* too, landing within 0.005 of the ceiling. Checked by eye as well as by score:
the golf image at 1.3 is unmistakably the same woman as the control portraits, where the
1.0 version was a different person.

`config/providers.yaml` now carries `lora_scale: 1.3` with these figures beside it, and
the evaluation script defaults to it rather than to 1.0.

**Not pushed higher on this evidence.** Three takes is still three takes, and
over-weighting a LoRA eventually burns the likeness into every output regardless of what
the prompt asks for. 1.3 is where the measurement supports, not where the trend points.

### What this changes about the conclusion below

The original recommendation — that more varied reference stills matter more than more
training — **still stands, but is no longer urgent**. At 1.3 the LoRA is usable for golf
content at roughly a two-in-three hit rate, which is workable with a review step. The
reference set remains 87% portraits and that ceiling has not moved; it is now a
throughput problem rather than a blocker.

One thing to watch that has nothing to do with identity: **the 1.3 golf image rendered a
Nike swoosh on the visor.** Real trademark, unprompted. That belongs in the content
policy guard's remit (task 3.2) before anything is published.

## What I would do next, cheapest first

1. **Re-run the golf prompt three times at $0.07 each.** One failure is not a rate. $0.21
   answers whether golf framing is reliably bad or was one bad draw.
2. **Try LoRA scale 1.1–1.3 on the same prompts.** Free to reason about, $0.56 to test,
   and a weak LoRA often just needs more weight before anything more expensive is worth
   trying.
3. **Only then consider 2000 steps ($48).** If the cause is the reference set being 87%
   portraits, more steps will train that bias harder rather than fix it.

The honest reading of finding 1 is that the next real improvement is **more varied
reference stills**, not more training. That is the same conclusion the body-coverage work
reached on 2026-09-29 and closed negatively at 3 of 8 — and it is now the second time
that gap has been the binding constraint.
