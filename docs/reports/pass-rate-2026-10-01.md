# Single-shot pass rate — 2026-10-01

Five independent runs of `scripts/make_single_shot.py` through the real production path,
after the first accepted shot, because one accepted shot is a single draw and the keyframe
screen exists precisely because single draws of a stochastic generator mislead (ADR 0011).

Total spend: **$5.176** (5 × $1.0352). No run errored; all five reached a verdict.

## Results

| run | outcome | keyframes passing | best keyframe | take coverage | time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | accepted | 1/4 | 0.96239 | 56–83% | 9.4m |
| 2 | accepted | 2/4 | 0.96707 | 71–98% | 9.8m |
| 3 | accepted | 1/4 | 0.96663 | 66–95% | 7.0m |
| 4 | accepted | 2/4 | 0.96576 | 68–93% | 5.1m |
| 5 | **gate refused** | 1/4 | **0.96731** | 63–98% | 9.0m |

**Pass rate: 4/5 = 80%.** Expected attempts per finished shot: **1.25**.

## The cost model the Phase 3 gate asks for

| | |

| per attempt | $1.0352 |
| per **finished** shot (÷ 0.80) | **$1.2940** |

This is generation spend and wall-clock only. **Operator time is not in here**, and the
Phase 3 gate explicitly wants it — *"operator time per finished piece is measured, because
labour is the real cost line."* It cannot be measured until the review UI (3.5) exists; a
human currently has no interface to spend time in.

## Finding 1 — best-of-four is carrying the pipeline

Only **7 of 20 individual keyframes (35%)** cleared the still threshold of 0.9619. A
pipeline generating one keyframe per shot would therefore refuse at the keyframe stage
about 65% of the time.

Taking the best of four leaves a **17.9%** chance that all four fail. Across five runs
that predicts about 0.9 keyframe refusals, and we saw 0 — consistent, and the sample is
far too small to say more than that.

The practical reading: the screen is not a safety net that rarely fires, it is load-bearing.
Four candidates at $0.07 are what make the $0.76 beneath them worth spending.

## Finding 2 — a passing keyframe does not predict a passing shot

Run 5 had the **highest** keyframe of all five runs (0.96731) and still failed the gate,
with 4 of 10 frames below the video threshold. Run 1 had the lowest (0.96239) and passed.

Across five runs the best-keyframe scores cluster in 0.9624–0.9673 — a span of 0.005 — and
within that band the keyframe score carries no signal about the outcome. ADR 0011's claim
is narrower than it might be read: screening the keyframe removes a *known* failure mode
(an off-identity first frame), it does not make the rest of the chain predictable.

**The variance that decides a run has moved downstream, into the animation.**

## Finding 3 — identity is paid for before it is checked

Run 5's chosen take, measured densely at 6fps *before* the sync:

```
take.mp4   mean 0.95531  min 0.92481  30/31 scorable (97%)  7 frames below 0.951
```

The take was already failing. The gate refusal was predictable for free, from a local
measurement, **before** $0.50 was spent syncing it. Takes are currently selected on head
roll and coverage only; identity is not scored until the final gate, after the sync.

This is the same shape as the finding behind ADR 0011 — a check sitting downstream of the
spend it should be gating.

### What this does *not* establish

I checked whether a different take would have saved run 5 and **cannot say**, because the
three losing takes are not scorable densely enough to compare:

```
take_0   9/31 scorable (29%)   too thin to judge
take_2   8/31 scorable (26%)   too thin to judge
take_3  12/31 scorable (39%)   too thin to judge
take.mp4 30/31 scorable (97%)  the one that was picked
```

Claiming the pick was wrong on those numbers would be the error this project has already
made once, when a verdict column reported one scorable frame in ten as "PASS every frame".
The honest statement is that the chosen take was measurably failing, not that a better one
was available.

### A discrepancy worth recording

`takes.measure()` reported 63–98% face coverage for run 5's takes. Dense identity sampling
found only 26–39% of frames scorable on three of them. Both go through dlib, so the two
coverage figures are measuring different things — `measure()`'s detector finds a face where
`read_still` will not produce an embedding from it.

That matters because `MIN_TAKE_COVERAGE` is enforced against the *first* number while the
gate needs the *second*. A take can clear the coverage floor and still be unscorable by the
gate. This is not the cause of any failure observed here, but the floor is not currently
measuring the thing it is protecting.

## Recommendation

Score identity on the takes before the sync, and select on it alongside roll and coverage.
It costs nothing — the measurement is local — and on run 5 it would have avoided $0.50 of
the $1.0352. Reconcile the two coverage measurements while doing it, since take selection
and the gate should not disagree about whether a take can be scored.

Both are extensions of ADR 0011 rather than new decisions, and neither needs new spend.

## Caveats

- **n=5.** An 80% pass rate from five runs has a wide interval; the true rate could
  plausibly sit anywhere from roughly 50% to 95%. It is enough to size a retry cap and a
  cost estimate, not enough to quote as a specification.
- All five runs used **one prompt, one shot type and one LoRA**. A different shot — wider
  framing, movement, a swing — is a different pass rate, and the Phase 2 battery already
  established that formats differ sharply.
- `MIN_TAKE_COVERAGE = 0.6` remains a floor chosen by analogy with the gate's threshold,
  not a calibrated number.
