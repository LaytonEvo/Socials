# Using a real video as the basis: what exists, read today

**Date:** 2026-10-13 · **Status:** research only. Nothing configured, nothing billed.
Supersedes the framing in `motion-transfer-options-2026-09-25.md`, which found the right
idea and the wrong endpoint.

The owner asked: *"There must be a model we can use OR use a real video as the basis/
reference of the video?"*

The answer to the second half is yes, and it is better than the motion-transfer route this
project costed in September.

## `minimax/h3-max/recast`

Read from fal's queue OpenAPI, 2026-10-13. fal's own description: *"Recast the people in a
video using reference photos with H3 Max, while preserving the source motion, camera,
composition."*

| Field | What it takes |
|---|---|
| `video_url` | **the real video**, 5–30s, no single shot over 15s |
| `reference_image_urls` | **1–4 photos**, one per person being replaced |
| `resolution` | `768P` or **`1080P`** |
| `enhance_realism` | *"more natural skin textures and better blending of the new people into the scene"* |
| output | *"the recast video, **with the source sound**"* |

**This inverts the problem.** Every golf fault of the last three weeks — the full swing
from 30 yards, the club missing the ball while the ball moves, the bag and trolley on the
green, two flagsticks, the hole cut in the fringe — is a property of a *scene the model
invented*. Recast does not invent the scene. The swing, the camera, the ball flight, the
course and its etiquette all come from footage of something that really happened. The only
synthetic thing in the frame is the person.

Four consequences, each of which closes something currently open:

1. **Golf correctness stops being a generation problem.** ADR 0016's swing measurement
   becomes a check on the *source footage* rather than a refusal of renders.
2. **1080P is a parameter.** The brief asks for 1080×1920 and the current chain gives
   768×1344.
3. **The source sound is kept.** Ambient and club sound have been on the unsolved list
   since September.
4. **Four reference photos, and the master set has 108 to pick from.** Identity is still
   the risk and is still measured by the existing gate — which is exactly the right shape
   of risk, because it is the one thing this project can measure.

### The alternative, and why it is second

`fal-ai/wan/v2.2-14b/animate/replace`, $0.08/s: *"integrate animated characters into
reference videos, replacing the original."* One reference image rather than four, and
maxes out at 720p. Same idea, less of it.

`fal-ai/kling-video/v3/{standard,pro}/motion-control` ($0.126 / $0.168 per second) remains
the motion-transfer option — character image plus driving video — but it transfers the
*motion* onto a generated scene, where recast keeps the scene as filmed. For golf, keeping
the scene is the whole point.

`fal-ai/id-v2v` ($0.20/s) restyles a video while preserving its subjects — the opposite of
what is wanted here.

## The price, verified 2026-10-15 — and it is not cheap

Read from the model's own record on `fal.ai/models/minimax/h3-max/recast`, its
`billingMessage` rather than the catalogue API:

> *"Billed by output video duration: **$0.30 per second at 768p and $0.45 per second at
> 1080p**. Reference images are included."*

**`api.fal.ai/v1/models/pricing` reports `0.05` with the unit `units` for this endpoint.**
That is six to nine times under the real figure. It is the starkest case yet of what ADR
0013 was written about: an unqualified number is not a price. The slot is configured at
the 1080P rate with `price_basis: {resolution: 1080P}` bound to what `request.base` sends,
so the config refuses to load if those ever part.

| | |
|---|---|
| 10-second pitch at 1080P | **$4.50** |
| 10-second pitch at 768P | $3.00 |
| 30-second source at 1080P | $13.50 |

For scale, the generation chain pays $0.04/s at 768P. **Recast is about eleven times that
per second** — and buys golf that is correct rather than golf that has to be refused. At
the $100/month ceiling a ten-second 1080P recast is 4.5% of the month, so this is
affordable to test and would need thought before it became routine.

## What is still NOT verified
- **Whether her face survives the recast.** The reason to run it, not a reason to assume.
- **How it handles a face that is only intermittently visible** — a golfer at address is
  looking down.

## What it needs, and the rights question

**A source video of a real person playing golf**, 5–30 seconds.

- **The owner films it.** Free, and the only option with no rights question: the footage is
  his own, and his likeness does not survive into the output. This was the conclusion on
  2026-09-25 and nothing has changed it.
- **Licensed stock.** The licence has to be read. Using a clip as an editorial *cutaway* is
  ordinary licensed use; feeding it to a model as generation input is the restricted case,
  and a growing number of stock licences name it specifically. Not the same question, and
  the second one is the one being asked here.
- **Footage of a real golfer from the internet.** No. Their likeness is the input, their
  performance drives the output, and neither is ours to use.

**Cutaways need none of this.** A ball rolling to a hole has no person in it, so it needs
no recast and no generation at all — it is a stock clip or a phone clip, cut straight in.
